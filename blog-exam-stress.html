STUDENT WELLNESS GUIDE

How to Manage Exam Stress — An Evidence-Based Guide

VERIFIED SOURCES aneev AI · Student Wellness Series · ~6 min read

Board exams, semester exams, competitive exams — stress is normal for students in India, but learning to manage it is essential. This guide isn't just "motivation" talk — every tip is based on research and official health sources (WHO, US CDC, US FDA, Government of India helplines).

Common signs of exam stress

  • Concentration breaking while studying, getting distracted again and again
  • Disturbed sleep — either can't fall asleep, or sleep too much
  • Burning in the stomach, acidity, or skipped meals
  • Irritability — getting angry or tearful over small things
  • The "I can't remember anything" feeling — a blank mind

1. Take breaks — pomodoro-style studying

25-50 minutes of focused study + 5-10 minute breaks. Scrolling your phone during a break doesn't count — walk, stretch, drink water, or rest with your eyes closed. Research shows spaced study with breaks produces better memory consolidation than long uninterrupted sitting.

2. 7-9 hours of sleep — non-negotiable

According to the US CDC, young adults aged 18-25 need 7-9 hours of sleep. All-nighters reduce recall — they don't improve it — because memory consolidation happens during sleep. Keep a fixed sleep-wake time (even on weekends), keep the room dark-cool-quiet, and switch off screens 1 hour before bed.

3. 20-30 minutes of exercise or a walk daily

Physical activity lowers cortisol (the stress hormone). You don't need a gym — a brisk walk, cycling, or skipping rope at home all work. For every hour of sitting, get up and walk for 3-5 minutes.

4. Understand your caffeine limits

According to the US FDA, healthy adults should have a maximum of 400 mg/day of caffeine (roughly 3-4 cups of coffee or 5-6 cups of tea). Avoid energy drinks and coffee at night — they worsen anxiety and disrupt sleep. Overdose signs: jitters, a racing heart, shaky hands.

5. Practice exams — the most underrated trick

Time-bound mock tests significantly reduce real exam anxiety — this is research-backed. Why? Because exam pressure becomes "normal" through practice. Before the real exam, do at least 3-5 full-length mocks under exam-like conditions (with a timer, phone away).

6. Don't skip meals

Skipping breakfast reduces concentration. Get protein daily — dal, chana, rajma, eggs, soya, curd — all affordable options. Sugar-heavy drinks and junk food cause energy crashes. Drink water mindfully — even mild dehydration worsens mood and focus.

7. Keep perspective — one exam is not your whole life

A major cause of stress: the "everything depends on this" thought. The honest truth: millions of people take exams every year, and there are many paths in life. Don't compare yourself with others — compare yourself only with who you were yesterday.

When seeking professional help is necessary

If these signs have lasted 2+ weeks — persistent low mood, loss of interest in everything, hopelessness, panic attacks, or thoughts of harming yourself — this isn't just "exam stress". Free and confidential help is available 24/7:

  • Tele-MANAS: 14416 — Govt of India, free, 20+ languages
  • KIRAN: 1800-599-0019
  • Talk to someone you trust — a parent, teacher, or counsellor

There is no shame in having a mental health problem — it is treatable.

Quick recap: breaks + 7-9 hours of sleep + daily exercise + caffeine limits + practice exams + proper meals + perspective. That's it — no magic, just consistency.
